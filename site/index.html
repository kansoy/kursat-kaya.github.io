Lissajous Figure

Two sinusoidal voltages drive the horizontal and vertical axes.

x = sin(3θ + φ), y = sin(2θ)

Theory Notes

The bright line is the selected phase. The fainter lines trace the preceding 60° of the sweep. Read more at Tektronix.

Model

Building a Square Wave

Add odd harmonics of a 1 kHz sine wave to approximate a square wave.

v(t) ≈ (4/π) Σ sin(kωt)/k

Theory Notes

More terms sharpen the edges, while a small overshoot persists near each jump. The sum runs over k = 1, 3, …, 2N − 1. Read more at MIT EECS.

Model

Charging a Capacitor

A 1 V step charges an initially empty capacitor through a 10 kΩ resistor.

v(t) = V[1 − exp(−t/RC)]

Theory Notes

At one time constant, τ = RC, the capacitor voltage reaches about 63.2% of its final value. Increase C to slow the response. Read more at MIT.

Model

Voltage from a Rotating Coil

A 100-turn coil of area 20 cm² rotates in a uniform 0.1 T field.

ε = −N dΦ/dt

Theory Notes

The magnetic flux through each turn is BA cos(ωt). Doubling the speed doubles both the frequency and the peak open-circuit voltage. Read more at MIT OpenCourseWare.
